How can a verb become an interaction?

I begin by exploring each verb’s etymology, connotations, and physicality, alongside the values and ideologies it carries. I then translate these layers of meaning into tangible interactions and feedback—something people can act on, experience, and respond to.

Across twenty studies of Sync, Tune, Drift, Inquire, and Reveal, I translate verbs into typography, motion, and interactive behaviour. These experiments explore visual communication, interaction design, and rapid prototyping through how an idea looks, moves, and responds.

Design Research

For each verb, I explore its origins and associations through four lenses: illustrative, abstract, emotive, and antithetical. Typography and visual references help me turn these readings into possible behaviours.

Tune: making harmony tangible

My research traced “tune” from musical pitch to adjustment, refinement, and putting a system in order. Sand Morph explores that search for harmony through a word made of grains—able to disperse, regroup, and take on a different order.

Tune / Sand Morph

The concept moves between two arrangements of the same material: lettering and a wave pattern. The transition disperses and regathers the grains, making the change in order visible rather than switching between static images.

Start with a word

Grains gather into TUNE, giving the abstract experiment a familiar point of entry.

Click to transform

The grains disperse and settle into a wave pattern. With sound enabled, the pattern is paired with a tone.

Return and explore

Click again to return to the word, then explore another pattern. Sound remains an optional layer.
