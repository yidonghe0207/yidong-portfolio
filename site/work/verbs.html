How can a verb become an interaction?

I begin by exploring each verb’s etymology, connotations, and physicality, alongside the values and ideologies it carries. I then translate these layers of meaning into tangible interactions and feedback—something people can act on, experience, and respond to.

Across twenty studies of Sync, Tune, Drift, Inquire, and Reveal, I translate verbs into typography, motion, and interactive behaviour. These experiments explore visual communication, interaction design, and rapid prototyping through how an idea looks, moves, and responds.

Design Research

For each verb, I explore its origins and associations through four lenses: illustrative, abstract, emotive, and antithetical. Typography and visual references help me turn these readings into possible behaviours.

Reveal: designing anticipation

The word’s origin in “unveiling” led me to the moment before something becomes clear. In Blur Reveal, I use partial visibility to suggest that there is something to uncover—and make the transition itself part of the experience.

I translated the glass study into a sequence of states. Hover makes the distortion respond; clicking stretches the bands horizontally until the word becomes clear.
